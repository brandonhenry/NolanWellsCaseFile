Evidence boundary

The public record fixes the general area, but not every recovery-stage detail.

The sheriff’s public timeline places authority notification around 8:45 AM and attributes the find to a National Park Service ranger near Horn Island’s northwest tip. MDMR’s packet records later CAD creation times. Public descriptions alternate between “in the water,” “at the shoreline,” and “on the beach.” Those phrases may describe different observation or recovery stages, but the currently obtained records do not establish their exact order.

Official and public anchors

The currently defensible July 6 chronology

  • Just before 6:00 AMPublic reporting says a United Cajun Navy aircraft was airborne.
  • Before 8:00 AMThe organization’s last search boat was reportedly underway.
  • Around 8:45 AMThe sheriff’s public timeline says authorities received a call that a National Park Service ranger found Nolan near Horn Island’s northwest tip. This is a notification time, not necessarily the exact discovery minute.
  • 9:11:09 AMMDMR created an assist-another-agency CAD call.
  • 10:07:36 AMMDMR created a deceased-person CAD call. The CAD creation times do not replace the earlier reported notification sequence.
  • Just after 11:00 AMWLOX reported that Nolan’s body reached the coroner’s office in Pascagoula.
  • Before 1:00 PMNolan’s family publicly confirmed his death.
  • About 2:00 PM · July 7Formal identification through dental records was reported. This was a separate milestone from the family’s July 6 confirmation.

Claim separation

What is supported—and what remains missing

Supported

General recovery area and later observations

  • Authorities were reportedly notified around 8:45 AM on July 6, 2026.
  • The reported recovery area was near Horn Island’s northwest tip.
  • MDMR created later assistance and deceased-person CAD calls.
  • Published aerial footage shows the northwest-tip area and personnel near the shoreline.
  • Thrasher describes what he says the search aircraft observed from above.
Not established

Exact discovery and recovery sequence

  • The exact discovery minute and authenticated coordinate.
  • Whether the first observation was in water, at the water’s edge, or on shore.
  • Whether anyone moved the body before the aerial observation.
  • The tide stage and precise shoreline position at each recovery step.
  • A complete chain from ranger discovery through law-enforcement and coroner transfer.

Records needed

The ranger report and scene record remain essential.

A complete National Park Service ranger report, original dispatch and notification logs, the authenticated recovery coordinate, scene photographs with metadata, tide and weather records, and a complete recovery-to-coroner chain would allow the public descriptions to be ordered and tested. Until then, “in the water,” “at the shoreline,” and “on the beach” should remain separately attributed descriptions—not collapsed into one verified scene narrative.
